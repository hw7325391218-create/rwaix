for Future Digital Commerce

11 October 2026  Version v6.4
RWAIX proposes a universal trust and transaction protocol built on NFTs and blockchain for digital commerce involving people, institutions, AI Agents, digital assets and physical devices. The protocol connects participant qualifications, transaction intent, contractual rights, performance evidence, acceptance, authorized settlement and dispute handling into a reviewable collaboration process, allowing different organizations to exchange records with explicit meaning for the same transaction. The company’s disclosed NFT business is the first planned implementation use case, connecting work and token bindings, rights and licences, orders, delivery acceptance and authorized payment. Compute facilities and other industry applications are subsequent extensions. This implementation sequence does not mean that the protocol is already deployed in production on the company’s platform.
This whitepaper proposes a transaction reference specification for industry adoption, defining shared semantics, core constraints, role-specific conformance and a validation path. Sections 4 to 5 set out proposed normative requirements; the other sections explain technical implementation, dual-token collaboration and application designs. Section 24 proposes a process for open evolution. Its current status is a proposed reference specification for implementation and review, rather than an industry standard adopted by a standards organization or validated across institutions. Existing company components and new development are disclosed separately in Section 23. Frozen machine-readable schemas, test packages and interoperability results are conditions for subsequent releases.
The dual-token design provides an economic profile with distinct functions for the transaction network. DCNFT records series-specific interests defined by contract for an identified facility SPV; $RWAIX is proposed for approved network fees, operator performance bonding, service incentives and limited parameter governance. Ordinary transaction objects, identity credentials, facility investment interests and the network token are modeled separately and connected through business identifiers and independent ledgers. General participants can verify or use services without being required to hold investment interests merely because they adopt this specification. Each economic module is enabled only when its own conditions are met.
This version retains the sourced industry context and adjustable technical-service billing model in Section 25, establishes the initial implementation sequence through the company NFT transaction path in Section 17, and provides an eight-scenario browser reference prototype with NFT transactions as the default entry point in Section 26. The prototype makes transaction commitments, evidence checks, separate acceptance and settlement across distinct ledgers observable. Implemented scope, recorded tests and outstanding production requirements are disclosed separately; they do not establish production deployment on the company’s platform, industry-standard status or full conformance certification.
1 The Technical Proposition for Future Digital Collaboration
The protocol addresses an evolving scenario in which digital systems progressively take on resource discovery, qualification checks, negotiation of terms, service invocation and task execution. Participants include people, businesses, devices and software agents with varying degrees of autonomy. As collaboration expands, systems need to establish who may make a commitment, whether evidence remains valid, whether an operation exceeds its authority and who handles errors. RWAIX designs shared interfaces around these questions.
The principle that everyone can establish trust and participate means that everyone has the opportunity, under public rules, to establish identity relationships, demonstrate specific qualifications or conduct, and collaborate while protecting privacy. Trust conclusions are limited to a proposition, purpose, time and set of dependencies; participants have routes to correction and appeal. The protocol does not generate a universal score of character or social value, nor does it treat NFT ownership as permanent proof of trustworthiness.
The universal trust core operates around four relationships: control between subjects and objects, provenance between claims and evidence, authorization between rights and operations, and correspondence between commitments and actual performance. NFTs provide typed object anchors; credentials and evidence express verifiable content; contextual policies determine whether a particular request meets its conditions; and independent executors constrain actual operations. Applications share these relationships while retaining their own sources of facts and accountability rules.
The future value of the technology depends on whether this capacity for collaboration remains consistent across applications: individuals carry qualifications, businesses verify suppliers, creators grant licences, Agents invoke services within limits, and devices provide evidence of maintenance and use. Technical advancement is validated through independent reviewability, security, privacy, interoperability and practical efficiency together. The company’s NFT transaction business provides the first planned complete implementation path; compute and other use cases subsequently test the scope of the same core.
The two instruments express specific project interests and network collaboration across applications separately. DCNFT holders derive their rights from the applicable series documents; permissions to use $RWAIX derive from approved network rules; qualifications and facts still require evidence verification. Participants may choose to provide services, verify credentials or use applications without becoming investors merely by participating in the universal trust network.
The commercial logic for industry use is to connect transaction commitments, performance facts and transfers of value in ways that different systems can understand: who commits, to whom performance is owed, what is delivered, when acceptance occurs, who may authorize payment and how errors are corrected must all be explicit. The protocol's value is demonstrated through replaceable service providers, portable evidence, accountable operations and measurable collaboration costs. A reference specification can form the basis for shared industry rules only when independent parties can implement the same version and recognize one another's results.
2 Proposed Technical Contributions and Existing Technologies
NFTs, digital signatures, decentralized identifiers, verifiable credentials, smart contracts and cryptographic proofs already provide a substantial technical foundation. Using these components does not itself establish originality. RWAIX's proposed contribution is to organize object identity, evidence dependencies, contextual policies, state changes and authorized execution into a protocol whose components can be verified together, and to test through different applications whether the same core is genuinely reusable.
The protocol proposes five research hypotheses: typed NFT trust objects can reduce semantic ambiguity across applications; a TrustReceipt carrying context and version information can be checked by independent implementations; an evidence dependency graph can identify circular endorsements and repeated counting of supporting evidence; credential lifecycle management and checks before execution can limit erroneous authorization after revocation; and open application adapters can reduce the need to modify core contracts when adding an industry. Section 22 sets out experimental design and counterexample testing requirements for these hypotheses.
W3C DID and VC standards provide models for identifier control and verifiable claims; ERC-721 and related standards provide on-chain object interfaces. EAS already provides schemas, on-chain and off-chain attestations, references, revocation and optional resolver rules; Sign Protocol's public documentation also positions it as a universal evidence layer. Building on these capabilities, RWAIX proposes explicit contracts for contextual evaluation and execution. Any significant advantage must be tested against a baseline that directly combines the existing components. Universal trust and evidence layers are established areas of work; this whitepaper does not treat a combination of components or a name as evidence of a global first. [S1–S5, S10–S11]
RWAIX uses the division of functions between DCNFT and $RWAIX as its proposed economic architecture, without requiring a new public blockchain or newly invented cryptographic algorithms. Protocol development first establishes evidence and permission mechanisms, then separately validates rights registration, network charging and incentive modules. Where token issuance conditions have not been met, service pilots can use other approved settlement methods. Any new cryptographic construction or consensus mechanism should separately publish its security model, analysis and review materials.
The incremental hypothesis of the dual-token design is that it can reduce the commingling of interests and operating funds while allowing industry adapters to reuse common fee and incentive interfaces. Validation should compare it with a single mixed-purpose token, separately accounted systems and a service baseline without token issuance, including the additional costs of contracts, quotes, liquidity and administration. The number of tokens is not itself evidence of technical advancement.
This version adds a hypothesis of consistent transaction semantics: given the same transaction terms, authorizations, evidence snapshot and versions, independent implementations can produce compatible states and reason codes and trace differences to explicit external dependencies. Validation covers normal delivery, partial performance, revocation, disputes, unknown payment outcomes and subsequent compensation. The objective is to reduce the cost of reinterpreting transaction meaning across institutions.
Section 26 turns selected research propositions into an interactive browser reference prototype for examining the relationships among fixed terms, signed evidence, authority and payment states. Executable examples provide a reviewable starting point. Advantages over existing approaches remain subject to the controlled comparisons and independent-implementation validation specified in Section 22.

3 Protocol Architecture and Trust Domains
RWAIX consists of a universal trust core, shared services and industry adapters. The core defines objects, credential references, evidence dependencies, policy versions, verification results and lifecycles. Shared services provide key recovery, evidence storage, indexing, verification and dispute handling. Industry adapters map specific business facts and contractual conditions into the core. Each industry adapter must disclose the external facts that the core cannot determine.
The identity and control layer manages control keys and delegated authority for people, organizations, devices and Agents. The NFT object layer provides stable anchors and explicit binding semantics for managed objects. The evidence layer organizes credentials, observations and provenance. The policy layer produces verification conclusions in a given context. The collaboration and execution layer advances commitments or payments using valid authorizations. Governance and interoperability span these layers, constraining versions, upgrades and messages across networks.
The chain stores only the minimum necessary object identifiers, version commitments, state roots and execution events. Sensitive source documents, raw telemetry and large evidence sets are generally held in controlled off-chain storage. A hash demonstrates consistency between particular bytes and a commitment; it does not establish that the content is true, that the data remains available or that an issuer has legal authority. Network finality, evidence authenticity and resolution of commercial disputes belong to different trust domains.
Verifiers may be operated by customers, partners or independent services. A common interface permits replacement of a verification service, but the relying party remains responsible for selecting trust roots, policies and dependencies. The core should support multiple implementations and exportable evidence packages, avoiding merely wrapping a single centralized account database in a blockchain interface.
The architecture adds a configurable dual-token adapter layer. DCNFT connects issuance series, rights documents and registers; $RWAIX connects fees, performance bonds, service incentives and limited governance. Separate ledgers maintain objects and evidence, facility interests and cash, and network fees and rewards, with linking identifiers for reconciliation. Adapters register an asset's network, contract address, precision, controlling roles and version. Tokens with the same name or cross-chain representations must not be assumed to convey the same legal rights.
The commercial transaction layer uses common transaction identifiers, obligation identifiers and event envelopes on top of this architecture, with industry adapters adding measurement, acceptance and legal conditions. One business transaction may involve multiple objects, performing parties and payment obligations. Evidence, contractual state and funds execution each retain their authoritative sources and are reconciled through consistency rules. Platforms can be replaced, but established obligations and historical evidence must not disappear when a system is migrated.

4 A Cross Industry Reference Specification for Commercial Transactions
This section defines a proposed transaction core for procurement, licensing, service delivery and other collaboration with identifiable parties and obligations. It establishes common semantics for recording and verification while preserving each industry's sources of facts, contractual arrangements and dispute processes. Proof-only processes and zero-price services may declare that no payment obligation exists. Financing, investment and electronic transferable documents still require separately defined rights and applicable conditions.
In Sections 4 to 5, the uppercase terms MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD and MAY express BCP 14 requirement levels: mandatory, prohibited, recommended subject to a justified exception, and optional, respectively. The uppercase keyword accompanying the Chinese text determines the requirement strength of this proposal; ordinary explanatory text does not thereby become a conformance assertion. The rules constrain only implementations claiming the corresponding version and role and do not give this proposal binding legal or industry-wide authority. [S22–S23]
4 1 Transaction Objects and Shared Semantics
N01 Versions and semantics. An implementation MUST pin protocolVersion, profileId, profileVersion, schemaDigest, tradeId and termsDigest for a transaction. TradeEnvelope references the parties and their roles, objects and units, obligations, acceptance rules, timing conditions, fee and payment arrangements, applicable documents and dispute routes. Each obligation has a stable obligationId. Changing the payment asset, chain or adapter for the same debt must not create an additional right to payment. Amounts use explicit currency or asset identifiers, precision and rounding rules; implicit units are prohibited.
N02 Parties and authority. An implementation MUST separately verify the basis for the roles of transaction parties, signatories, service beneficiaries, payers, payees and verifiers, binding authorityRef, scope of authority, validity period and revocation status. Wallet control, a platform account, an Agent signature or token holdings alone cannot replace the required commercial authority. A missing mandatory condition returns a denial or indeterminate result.
N03 Formation and amendment of terms. The system MUST record evidence that authorized parties have consented to a specific termsDigest, together with the applicable conditions for effectiveness. Quotes, orders, invoices and delivery acknowledgments retain their distinct business meanings; an invoice alone does not establish that goods have been accepted. An amendment creates a new version and obtains the required consent under the specified rules, preserving the prior version and its outstanding obligations. The technical state COMMITTED means only that the commitment conditions specified by the profile have been recorded. Contract formation and legal effect remain dependent on the applicable facts and law. The UNCITRAL Model Law on Automated Contracting, adopted in 2024, distinguishes attribution of a system's outputs or actions from their legal consequences. It provides a model for legislation, rather than globally applicable contract rules. [S26]
4 2 Transaction Lifecycle and Multiple State Dimensions
N04 States and events. An implementation MUST bind each TradeEvent to eventId, tradeId, obligationId, eventType, actor, authorityRef, streamId, sourceId, prevEventDigest, sequence, causalRefs, effectiveAt, recordedAt, evidenceRefs and a signature domain. The schema must explicitly specify when an obligation identifier is inapplicable. effectiveAt is the asserted effective time; recordedAt is the time the system receives and records the event. They are not interchangeable. State reconciliation uses the ordering, conflict rules and authority conditions fixed by the profile; the last message to arrive must not overwrite a previously valid state merely because it arrived last. sequence and prevEventDigest are scoped to the same signed event stream; causalRefs expresses dependencies across streams. State reconciliation must validate event authority, expected versions and causality. The sequence numbers or timestamps of individual parties must not be treated as a global event order.
Transactions maintain at least five separate dimensions: commitment, performance, payment execution, receipt and discharge, and disputes. Repeated events are processed idempotently by business identifier, out-of-order events remain pending reconciliation, and conflicts follow an explicit pause or review path. Financing-interest registration and network rewards are recorded separately. A successful chain transaction updates only the corresponding execution fact; it must not simultaneously imply acceptance, receipt of funds and resolution of a dispute.

4 3 Delivery Acceptance and Separately Authorized Settlement
N05 Delivery and acceptance. An implementation MUST separately retain the delivery claim, supporting evidence, verification receipt and the AcceptanceRecord of an authorized acceptance party, fixing the object version, quantity, quality metrics, acceptance deadline and objection rules. TrustReceipt establishes only the result of verification against specified rules. Automatic acceptance requires prior agreement on its trigger conditions and authority; neither a lack of response nor a successful on-chain write may by itself constitute acceptance. Delivery in batches and partial acceptance use separate obligations or explicitly defined subscopes. Partial acceptance uses a unique portionId to bind its subscope, quantity and allocation to a payable. Repeated acceptance records for the same delivery scope must not count quantities or create payables more than once.
N06 Separate funds execution. Payment MUST be triggered by effective payment terms and current authority. Advance payments, installments and final payments due on acceptance each identify their trigger conditions. For the same obligationId, confirmed discharge and pending payments that could still take effect must not together exceed the approved payable amount. OUTCOME_UNKNOWN retains the reservation for reconciliation. Refunds, compensation and reversals create new obligations or adjustment records referencing the original record and execute under appropriate authority; they do not delete the original payment fact. Within the funds dimension, an implementation MUST record paymentInstructionState, fundsReceiptState and obligationDischargeState separately and retain the basis for each confirmation. CONFIRMED refers only to the confirmed execution record. Receipt of funds, the amount discharged and reversibility are reconciled separately under the contract, payment channel and applicable rules; chain confirmation must not replace those determinations. Amounts confirmed on the payment rail but awaiting attribution of the received funds or reconciliation of discharge continue to count against the original obligation's payment limit. Each amount is assigned to one mutually exclusive state category under a unique paymentAttemptId; execution confirmation must not release allowance before discharge is recorded. The amount counted against the limit includes discharged amounts, executed payments awaiting attribution and other payments that could still take effect. Their total must not exceed the approved payable amount.
DCNFT connects facility-series rights documents and actual distributable cash; $RWAIX connects approved network fees, bonds and rewards. Customer purchase payments, facility financing, network fees and node rewards each have their own creditors and funding basis. The core transaction interface permits other payment methods under lawful configurations. Asset transfers may be described as atomic exchanges only within the same executable atomic boundary. Processes spanning chains, banks and physical delivery must state their timing gaps, failure handling and compensation mechanisms rather than advertise end-to-end risk-free atomic settlement.
4 4 Dispute Remedies and Preservation of Records
N07 Disputes and remedies. An implementation MUST record in DisputeRecord the initiating party, affected obligations and amounts, reasons, evidence deadlines and supplementation rules, decision-making authority, notices and routes to remedy. Pausing, releasing bonds, imposing penalties and adjusting payments each require verification of authority. Separable undisputed obligations continue under their own rules. Timeout handling requires an explicit basis agreed in advance; silence must not automatically be interpreted as an admission of breach.
On-chain technical finality, settlement confirmation and legal finality are recorded separately. RESOLVED means only that the designated process has addressed the matter within its authority and scope; it does not necessarily preclude objections, review or remedies under applicable law. A terminated or settled transaction can still receive appended records arising from a valid dispute, correction of an error or a decision of a competent authority. Corrections preserve the original evidence and complete reference relationships. Electronic transferable records involve additional requirements concerning reliability, integrity and control. An NFT identifier or on-chain holding alone cannot establish their legal effect; the relevant profile should be determined separately for the local legislation and specific document. [S25] MLETR is a model law; its 2017 text excludes securities such as shares and bonds, and other investment instruments. This protocol does not use it as a basis for DCNFT issuance, transfer or legality.
N08 Data and recoverability. An implementation MUST limit collection and disclosure and define evidence access, retention periods, handling of legally required deletion or preservation, and recovery procedures. Public logs use necessary commitments and controlled references; avoidable sensitive source content must not be written to them. A signature or hash does not ensure data availability. When evidence needed for verification cannot be recovered, the system should report an indeterminate or unverifiable result rather than represent it as valid proof.
5 Interoperability and Conformance Testing
Conformance claims are limited to a specific version, profile, role and module. Verifiers, executors, industry adapters and economic modules have different responsibilities. Selecting an optional module does not remove its dependent core requirements. DCNFT and $RWAIX form RWAIX's economic design; basic transaction and evidence interoperability must not require token holdings or investment subscriptions by default.
5 1 Role Scope and Version Negotiation
N09 Interoperability with Explicit Semantics. A receiving party MUST check both parties' capability manifests, versions, critical extensions and semantic mappings. Unsupported required fields or conditions must be explicitly rejected rather than silently discarded or automatically downgraded. The behavior of optional extensions may be ignored, while the original signed content is retained under the signature rules. Exchanges across industries must preserve roles, rights types, currencies and units, acceptance conditions, dispute states and original provenance.
Verifiers are responsible for evidence, status and policy results; executors for current authority, funds or operational boundaries, and reconciliation; industry adapters for mapping external facts and identifying what they cannot guarantee; and economic modules for the relevant rights, fee, bond and reward rules. An implementation may perform only one of these roles, but must declare functionality it does not implement. A verification service's conformance report cannot substitute for conclusions about an executor or asset issuer.
5 2 Rule Vectors and Reviewable Tests
N10 Evidence of conformance. An implementation claiming conformance to a frozen profile of this proposal MUST provide a ConformanceManifest identifying specification and profile versions, a code or build digest, roles, enabled modules, dependencies and trust roots, the test-package version, results and known limitations. MUST requirements cannot be recorded as passed through an exemption. Deviations from SHOULD requirements must explain their reasons and interoperability impact. Modules not yet implemented or tested are marked as unevaluated.
Test vectors fix inputs, authority and state snapshots, expected events and results, permitted side effects and prohibited changes. The matrix below gives the minimum categories of negative tests. A complete test package must also cover valid paths, interactions at boundaries, concurrency and recovery. A conformance claim for a specified scope requires every applicable mandatory assertion in the same frozen test set to pass; this does not establish the absence of unknown attacks. On-site claims with valid signatures but false content require industry sampling to assess false-acceptance rates; format tests alone cannot guarantee their detection.

5 3 Exchange Across Implementations and Release Conditions
An interoperability candidate release SHOULD involve at least two independent implementation teams, each completing transaction creation, evidence exchange, verification, state replay and recovery from exceptions for the same transaction. Calling a shared backend does not constitute independent implementation. Initial interoperability testing covers three profiles: NFT transactions; work provenance and licensing; and institutional qualifications and Agent delegation, with fixed examples involving mixed fees and partial failure. NFT transactions lead the sequence; compute and other scenarios follow when their domain requirements are met. Three pilots cannot establish that every scenario has been validated.
Release materials include implementable schemas and interfaces, profile and extension registries, complete vectors, exportable event and evidence packages, known issues, discrepancy reports and migration rules. Interface publication, specification licences, code licences and patent commitments are disclosed separately. Unresolved ownership rights must not be described as an open licence. Self-declaration, independent assessment and adoption by a standards organization are different levels of evidence; their reports cannot substitute for one another.
6 NFT Trust Objects and Binding Semantics
An NFT is an on-chain anchor for a programmable trust object. Its object reference is determined jointly by chainId, contractAddress, tokenId and bindingVersion; its description specifies meaning through schemaId, contentDigest, controller, subjectBinding, rightsDescriptorRef and lifecyclePolicy. NFT metadata updates create a new version and event; they must not silently change, within the same version, the semantics on which past verification relied. Update rules are constrained by the selected standard: metadata of an issued ERC-5484 token must not be changed. Changes must instead follow the specified burn-and-reissue process or use newly referenced credentials; bindingVersion cannot circumvent the restriction. [S7]
Token holders, control keys, claim subjects, service beneficiaries and legal rights holders must be distinguished. The holder record under ERC-721 describes token-level state; it does not automatically establish a natural person's identity, copyright in a work or ownership of a physical asset. The creation and transfer of legal rights depend on applicable documents, registrations and authority. The protocol explicitly references these foundations rather than compressing different relationships into a single owner field. [S4] A unique tokenId within one contract also cannot prevent another contract or chain from describing the same physical object. Registration rules must identify known duplicates and conflicts and specify authoritative sources and dispute handling.
Objects bound to a subject should use a configuration that prohibits ordinary transfers or permits only a strictly controlled recovery process. Asset objects may be transferable under the applicable conditions; transfer of a service commitment or substitution of its obligations requires the relevant parties' consent. Historical evidence attached to a transferable asset does not establish the new holder's personal qualifications. Every transfer requires reassessment of current dependencies and authority; historical versions and their subject relationships at the time remain traceable.
A subject anchor should expose only necessary identifiers. Sensitive identities can use different identifiers in different relationships, controlled mappings and selective disclosure. Public identity NFTs may enable correlation across platforms, so no person should be required to mint a permanent public real-name record. A person may manage multiple identifiers for authorized purposes, and devices and organizations may have independent objects. Counts of wallets or NFTs alone cannot establish the uniqueness of natural persons.
DCNFT is a specialized object category with separately defined contractual interests; the term must not be applied to every NFT trust object. Facility identity NFTs, personal qualifications, work licences and device records continue to operate under their own types. Only rights created through effective documents can change through a compliant transfer. The DCNFT issuer, seriesId, rightsDocumentDigest, register reference, transfer conditions and registration time must be bound to the object version, with separate audit trails for key recovery and corrections to rights records.

7 Dual Token Collaboration and Economic Operations
The dual-token design expresses interests in a specific facility separately from network collaboration across applications, connecting them through the same evidence and authorization core. DCNFT is a non-fungible or series-based representation of interests, initially applied to contractual participation interests in facility SPVs. $RWAIX is a proposed fungible network token for approved fees, performance bonding, service incentives and limited governance. Dual-token refers to two economic functions; it does not mean that every service must use both instruments or that every NFT carries investment rights.
The collaboration path runs from effective rights and service commitments through verifiable evidence, approved fees or rewards, independent execution and ledger reconciliation. DCNFT and $RWAIX have no default conversion, redemption, mutual guarantee or claim on each other's cash flows. Returns on investment interests depend on the corresponding effective documents and funds actually available. Network-service continuity depends on deliverable services and customer demand; token appreciation must not be treated as a foundational source of payment capacity.

7 1 Rights Binding and Authorized Transfers
Each DCNFT series binds the actual issuing SPV, rights-document version, register, holder eligibility, economic priority, term, default remedies and applicable transfer restrictions. The working assumption remains contractual participation linked to facility distributable cash. The final choice of equity, debt or another instrument must be reflected consistently in the schema and signed documents. An NFT transfer produces the corresponding legal effect only when the applicable contractual, registration and eligibility conditions are satisfied.
Token quantities held and distribution weights need not coincide. The distribution engine calculates amounts using the record date, eligible register, weights, deductions and approved amount specified in the series documents, retaining bank or custody receipt records, debt constraints and payment receipts. The platform establishes no joint payment obligation across facilities, default group guarantee or distribution ratio that can be rewritten by a $RWAIX vote. New allocations use eligible cash that has not yet been allocated as their base. Amounts already allocated to investors and residual rights holders in a prior period or the current period are excluded. If the base already excludes these amounts, they are not deducted again; actual payments only discharge the corresponding payable entitlements.
7 2 Fee Quotes and Separately Accounted Settlement
Service charges and network fees are defined separately. FeeQuote binds quoteId, quoteUnit, feeAsset, maxDebit, the payee, rateSource, rateTime, expiry and refundRule. A fee asset is uniquely identified by network, contract and precision; fiat uses an explicit currency. When payment in $RWAIX is selected, the quote specifies the conversion direction, responsibility for fees and permitted deviation. A stale, abnormal or unavailable price source pauses that payment path rather than permitting an unapproved increase in the debit or substitution of another asset. Acceptance of a quote fixes the settlement quantity or specifies the conversion rule and maximum debit. Exceeding an approved deviation requires a new quote and new authorization; previously consumed allowance must not be recalculated and released because prices change.
Fee execution follows quote acceptance, allowance reservation, authorized submission, outcome reconciliation and settlement or refund. OUTCOME_UNKNOWN retains the reservation while the original transaction is queried. Facility funds, platform service fees, the network treasury, bonds and reward budgets are accounted for separately. Service-revenue deductions, debt and reserves are verified against series documents and financial records before distributable cash is determined. Token market value, unsold allocations and unconfirmed receivables are not cash received. The same service obligation shares a unique obligationId across payment assets and is reconciled using a fixed basis for measuring discharge. Confirmed payments and pending payments that could still take effect must not together exceed the approved amount payable. Changing the asset or transaction identifier creates no new payment authority.
7 3 Performance Bonds and Service Incentives
Performance bonds use BondRecord to record the responsible party, obligation, bond asset, locked amount, dispute window and authority to release or deduct funds. A bond supports performance obligations; it does not establish factual truth or automatically bear facility debt. If the bond asset is $RWAIX, its price and liquidity changes require stress testing. A shortfall limits new obligations or triggers additional protection under predefined rules; it must not be shifted to existing rights holders without authority.
RewardClaim binds a unique service, provider, verification result, amount and asset, and budget period. It enters claimable and claimed states only after deduplication, conflict checks, the approved dispute process and budget approval. Locking a bond does not itself produce a fixed return. Service compensation and separately approved staking incentives are disclosed separately. Unapproved rewards, buybacks, burns or APR are not presented as return commitments, and funds from new participants must not pay previously guaranteed returns. Reward sources are identified separately as earned service fees, approved subsidies or approved emissions. Amounts allocated but unclaimed, and amounts whose payment outcome is unknown, continue to consume the cumulative budget. A timeout, address change or cross-chain movement must not release that budget or credit the reward again.
7 4 Supply Governance and Sustainable Operations
The cap of 1,000,000,000 tokens and allocation from v3.3 are retained as a candidate design budget, not approved issuance or allocation. Amounts in the table are design ceilings. Sales, vesting and releases do not begin until the issuer, legal holders, recipient conditions, distribution routes, release schedules and final treatment of unearned allocations have been determined and approved. Team allocations, liquidity-wallet balances, unlocked amounts and actual circulating supply are disclosed separately. Cross-chain representations must not increase the unified economic supply.
$RWAIX governance operates only within authorized parameter scopes, with rules for proposals, eligibility, voting participation thresholds, conflicts, delayed execution and emergency protection. Voting does not directly control SPV funds or unilaterally change DCNFT weights, priority, security or remedies. Changes to supply, fee and reward parameters are constrained by the relevant legal entity and technical permissions together, with version and execution records retained.
Long-term economic sustainability should be driven by real service demand: customers pay agreed charges, eligible services receive compensation, and approved network fees support reconcilable operating expenditure. Reward budgets may support service development, but their sources, consumption rates and subsidy dependence must be disclosed. Acceptance testing also models cash needs and service costs without token appreciation or new financing and with reduced rewards, avoiding artificial demand created through circular incentives.

8 Identity Qualifications and Open Participation
The identity layer supports natural persons, organizations, devices and software agents, but does not equate proof of key control with proof of a subject's identity. A device signature can demonstrate possession of a device key, and an organizational statement can demonstrate that an issuer made a claim. Real-world identity, institutional registration, qualifications and device ownership require their own evidence and verification processes. A DID is a method of identification and control; it does not automatically provide identity vetting, Sybil resistance or a trustless authority. [S1–S2]
Open participation is differentiated by activity. Browsing public specifications, developing adapters, verifying public proofs and submitting authorized evidence should not generally require investment eligibility. Issuing professional qualifications, receiving restricted data, controlling devices, handling funds or participating in regulated transactions requires the separate permissions and conditions appropriate to that activity. Participation by anyone does not mean that everyone may perform every operation.
Access can include ordinary wallets, smart accounts, access through institutional intermediaries and interfaces designed to lower technical barriers. Fee sponsorship, mobile workflows, multiple languages and recoverable accounts can reduce participation costs; sponsorship limits, rate limits, abuse controls and intermediaries' authority must be transparent. Depending on the product configuration, participants should be able to verify public objects and offline evidence without a native token or paid staking. Recovery, session permissions and budgets for smart accounts still require implementation by the specific account; adopting ERC-4337 alone does not establish those capabilities. [S9]
Credential issuers' authority must have a scope and validity period. The protocol can record an issuer's institutional affiliation, permitted scope and status, but deciding which issuer to accept remains a policy choice tied to the intended use. For participants unable to supply a particular credential, applications should explain alternative evidence routes, functional limits and appeal procedures, rather than interpreting missing evidence as personal untrustworthiness.
Role eligibility and token holdings are verified separately. Ordinary users may establish identities and submit proofs under the applicable rules. Node roles with economic responsibilities may be subject to approved performance-bond requirements, while DCNFT purchasers undergo separate offering and transfer eligibility checks. $RWAIX holdings, voting power or staked amounts cannot replace qualification evidence or generate a personal trustworthiness score.
9 Claims Evidence and Dependency Graphs
CredentialEnvelope stores issuer, subjectRef, claimsDigest, schemaId, validFrom, validUntil, statusRef and proofSuite. Its source document may be a verifiable credential, a signed attestation or an adapted industry-specific proof. EvidenceBundle associates observationRef, source, captureTime, integrityProof, availabilityPolicy and relevant credentials. Adapters must distinguish observed facts, issuer claims, third-party opinions and algorithmic inferences. A custom Envelope is a container; it may be described as a conforming VC only when the complete profile of the selected standard is satisfied. Adaptation must not discard the original signature, subject or status semantics. [S2]
Typed edges in the evidence graph express who issued a statement, which observation it relies on, which action it authorizes, which version it supersedes and who reviewed it. Nodes should carry identifiers for provenance and controlling parties. The same original evidence repackaged by several intermediaries must not automatically count as multiple independent sources. A cycle of mutual endorsements cannot generate its own root of trust; verification rules should include cycle detection, limits on dependency depth and clustering of parties under common control. Provenance relationships may record historical cycles, but the dependency subgraph used to derive a verification conclusion must be acyclic and subject to limits on node count, edge count and evaluation depth. Violations return explicit errors.
Facts about the physical world depend on their input paths. Asset existence, delivery of goods, device location and human conduct may require on-site inspection, independent observations or dispute procedures. The protocol preserves observation methods, device identities, signature times and anomaly flags so that verifiers can see the trust assumptions. Consensus can consistently record an incorrect input; multiple sensors controlled by the same party do not constitute independent evidence.
An evidence package must include the formats, algorithm identifiers, versions and retrieval conditions needed to replay verification. If data is unavailable, beyond its retention period or inaccessible to the verifier, the result should identify the resulting capability gap. On-chain anchoring does not replace availability safeguards. Retention periods, redundancy, access permissions, backups and deletion responsibilities form part of the deployment contract.
Economic events must state their evidentiary scope when added to the evidence graph: a DCNFT register event supports a series holding relationship, a fee receipt supports a confirmed payment event, and reward verification supports a specific service contribution. Wash trading, circular calls or mutual endorsements among affiliated nodes must not earn rewards directly on the basis of transaction volume. Evidence for the same service is deduplicated using a unique business identifier, with known common-control relationships identified.
10 Contextual Policies and Portable Trust Receipts
RWAIX uses TrustContext to specify the audience, purpose, object, requested action, jurisdiction or business domain, challenge nonce, deadline and policy version for a verification request. TrustPolicy is a fixed-version rule set defining permitted issuers, required predicates, evidence freshness, necessary independent sources and failure handling. Instead of blending all evidence into a global score, the protocol preserves individual evaluations and their dependencies.
Verification first checks structure and signatures, then subject bindings, issuer authority, status, time, context and evidence dependencies, before running a deterministic policy. For the same normalized input, policy version and state snapshot, conforming implementations should produce the same result. Live network queries must be converted into input snapshots with provenance and timestamps; otherwise, different machines cannot be expected to reproduce the original decision fully. The verifier first creates a separate PolicyDecision that fixes ruleSetDigest, engineVersion, engineBuildDigest, inputSnapshotDigest, dependencyGraphDigest, predicateResults and reasonCodes, then includes its decisionRef in the receipt. Rule changes, additional inputs and human adjudication each produce a new decision rather than overwriting the previous result. Evaluation must not implicitly read the live clock, random values or model outputs; any necessary external values are supplied as fixed inputs. Successful historical replay does not establish that execution is still permitted now.
TrustReceipt records contextDigest, policyDigest, decisionRef, evidenceRoot, objectVersion, statusSnapshotRef, stateReference, evaluatedAt, validUntil, predicateResults, decision, reasonCodes, verifierRef and a signature. ALLOW means that the request satisfies the rules under the specified dependencies; DENY means that a failure to satisfy a requirement has been established; INDETERMINATE means that essential facts or evidence are insufficient. States such as EXPIRED, REVOKED and DISPUTED are recorded as explicit reasons and dependency states and must not be treated as ALLOW.
A portable receipt can be used in two ways: the relying party can accept the verifier's signed conclusion, or independently replay verification if authorized to obtain the necessary evidence. The former still trusts the verifier; the latter still trusts the accepted evidence roots and observation methods. A zero-knowledge proof can establish that specified predicates or computations satisfy constraints. Its applicable circuit, public inputs, trusted setup and privacy leakage must be explicit. A valid proof does not establish facts outside the circuit's coverage.
Dual-token adapters bind applicable fields such as tokenRef, seriesId, rightsDocumentDigest, quoteId and rewardClaimId to the context, version and evidence snapshot, explicitly omitting fields that do not apply. A TrustReceipt may state which rules a particular rights-transfer or fee request satisfies, but does not itself change the register, debit funds, establish reward entitlement or allocate cash. Those actions still require separate authorization and confirmation of final state.

11 Privacy Minimal Disclosure and Revocable Status
Privacy design begins with data minimization: identity documents, contact details, original contracts, sensitive business data and plaintext that directly reveals identity do not belong on a public chain. Hashing is not anonymization. Low-entropy fields may be enumerated through dictionary attacks, while public timestamps, addresses and relationship graphs may also reidentify subjects. Commitments require appropriate randomness; indexes and access logs also belong in the privacy threat model.
Selective disclosure reveals only the fields needed for the current purpose. With an appropriate proof suite, predicate proofs can replace complete records for propositions such as age or qualification thresholds. Proof methods differ in linkability, computational cost and support for revocation. Each protocol profile fixes its security suite and verification procedure; it cannot assume that every credential inherently supports hiding arbitrary fields or generating zero-knowledge proofs.
Status checks must balance freshness and privacy. Batch status lists, caching or proven status snapshots can reduce the risk of revealing a subject's activity through individual queries. List update times, permitted staleness windows and handling of status-service failures must still be defined. High-risk operations should pause or move to human review when status is uncertain, rather than silently falling back to acceptance. When using a Bitstring Status List, the list's signature, purpose, index, validity period and issuer authority must be verified. Irreversible revocation and reversible suspension are handled separately. [S3]
Revocation, suspension or expiry requires future dependency evaluations to be updated; it does not erase history. Earlier receipts retain the rules and status snapshots used at the time, while new operations recheck current status. Off-chain deletion must also address copies and access permissions. Complete deletion of existing commitments on a public chain cannot be promised, so sensitive data should remain off-chain from the outset.
DCNFT investor eligibility, holdings and distribution data, together with node bond and reward data, are subject to role-based minimal disclosure. Revoking a qualification can block subsequent operations relying on it; it does not automatically confiscate tokens, erase valid claims or cancel earned compensation. Freezes, deductions, recovery and dispute handling each require their own basis and authority. Correlation risks from public-chain holdings and fee records should be included in privacy testing.
12 Lifecycle State Safety and Account Recovery
The object lifecycle uses DRAFT, ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, REVOKED and RETIRED. SUSPENDED may return to an active state under the rules. REVOKED and RETIRED do not reactivate the original version; reestablishing a relationship requires a new version or object while preserving history. When the credential layer maps to a Bitstring Status List, revocation is irreversible and suspension is reversible; renewed qualifications require credential reissuance under the issuer's rules. Minting, transferring or burning an NFT is a token event and does not automatically constitute credential issuance, contract effectiveness or extinguishment of rights. [S3]
Verification and execution occur at different times. Before execution, the executor must recheck the receipt's purpose, validity period, nonce, object version, current permissions and required status freshness, preventing erroneous authorization where revocation occurs after verification but goes unchecked before execution. On the same chain, an atomic operation can check critical versions and check and consume the nonce. Dependencies on off-chain state require a stated maximum delay and pause conditions; the time window cannot be described as entirely eliminated.
Account recovery should combine mechanisms such as multiple factors, approval by several parties, delayed effectiveness, notifications and dispute freezes. Recovering control keys does not automatically restore revoked qualifications, transfer personal identity or transfer another person's assets. Separate version relationships are created before and after recovery so that verifiers can identify key rotation, controller changes and any need to reissue credentials. Procedures should allow withdrawal or freezing in response to misconduct by an authorized recovery party. For ERC-1271 contract accounts, records must also identify the historical signature-verification block, account implementation and permission version. Current signature-verification success or failure alone cannot reconstruct past validity. [S8]
Chain reorganizations, duplicate cross-chain messages and service retries require unique event keys, a nonce, prior-state conditions, idempotent handling and finality confirmation. Cross-chain and off-chain operations use explicit pending-confirmation states, timeouts and compensation procedures. Even if all components are eventually consistent, unconditional atomic settlement across multiple independent networks and banks cannot be claimed. Chain-event records distinguish submission, inclusion in a block, satisfaction of configured finality conditions and removal by reorganization; blockHash and a confirmation policy fix the snapshot. Critical authorizations do not rely on events that have not met finality conditions, and pending operations affected by a reorganization are reconciled again.
A single business process records object state, rights-registration state, token-transfer state and funds state separately. Transfer submission, on-chain confirmation, an update to a statutory or contractual register and receipt of cash are different events. Court orders, key recovery or registration corrections append records through authorized processes rather than delete the original history. Cross-chain representations maintain a unified economic supply through locking or burning, minting and replay protection.
13 Verification Networks and Collusion Resistance
The verification network is open through replaceable specifications and implementations. Applications may verify for themselves or select recognized independent verifiers. Node registration establishes only that a node has satisfied the registration process; professional capability, accountability and independence still require explicit qualifications and oversight. The protocol should disclose major dependencies, service availability and concentration rather than presenting multiple nodes under common control as decentralization.
Verification by multiple parties improves reliability only when its threat-model assumptions hold. Critical facts may require different controlling parties, different observation paths and separation of roles. Random assignment, cross-review, conflict disclosures and signed records provide supporting controls. Reaching a signature threshold does not demonstrate economic or organizational independence, nor does it rule out contamination of a shared input.
Staking, challenge bonds or insurance can create economic constraints for certain adjudicable violations, but capital cannot purchase truth. Slashing conditions should be explicit, verifiable and open to appeal; subjective disputes that cannot be objectively determined should not trigger automatic penalties. Verification of public facts, restricted professional judgments and legal adjudication require different accountability arrangements. No universal vote can replace every professional and judicial process.
The evidence graph can identify known common control and circular dependencies, but cannot guarantee discovery of hidden collusion. Evaluation must cover malicious issuers, bribed verifiers, compromised keys, colluding participants and honest but malfunctioning nodes, reporting false acceptance, false rejection, processing latency and the cost of human intervention.
$RWAIX performance bonds constrain defined node responsibilities; a larger stake does not make evidence more truthful. Verifier selection should combine independence, task capability, sampling and conflict checks, none of which can be replaced by bond size. Service rewards should depend on deduplicated eligible contributions and approved budgets, with tests for Sybil accounts, affiliated-party collusion and reward capture. Unearned allocations do not create automatic income rights.
14 Constrained Agents and Executable Collaboration
An Agent is a software participant with delegated authority. Every request should identify its principal, target object, authorized purpose, permitted tools, monetary or resource limits, validity period and audit reference. Natural language generated by a model does not directly confer transaction authority. A deterministic executor validates structured intents and, where necessary, requires review or approval by the responsible party. Subdelegation may only narrow actions, duration and limits; it must not expand authority, renew itself or reset the principal's shared budget. The delegation chain and its revocations are also execution dependencies.
Budgets and permissions cannot be checked only per request. Concurrent Agents, retries and split transactions must share a limit ledger that can be updated atomically, constraining cumulative amounts, rates and resource use. External documents, webpages, credential attachments and tool outputs are untrusted inputs; instructions within them cannot increase an Agent's authority or change a payee. Within the same authorization scope, asset and measurement period, reservations include the agreed fee upper bound. Consumed allowance plus pending reservations must not exceed the total allowance. Durable records link reservations, execution confirmations and releases to the same operation.
Collaborative commitments may use business states such as PROPOSED, ACCEPTED, IN_PROGRESS, EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED, FULFILLED, DISPUTED and CANCELLED, with transition conditions defined by a profile accepted by the parties. An ALLOW receipt provides evidence only that the rules have been satisfied; the actual executing party must still possess the necessary authority. Acceptance, recognition of a payable, payment approval and confirmation of receipt of funds are recorded separately.
Operations involving funds, digital objects or devices use their respective execution adapters, which enforce checks on prior state, nonce, beneficiary, limits, version and expiry. Results reference the original intent and receipt. Failures, rollbacks and reversals append records rather than overwrite history. Applications need not involve payment, and the protocol does not require all trusted collaboration to be financialized. A timed-out external request enters OUTCOME_UNKNOWN and awaits status queries and reconciliation, retaining its reserved allowance while its status is queried under the original operation identifier. The corresponding allowance is released only after non-execution is confirmed or verifiable compensation is completed. A timeout must not be treated as non-execution followed by direct submission of a new request. Before execution, persist the intent and a pending-send record; retries or queries use the same idempotency identifier. If the downstream system does not support idempotency and status queries, apply limits, manual reconciliation or a more conservative execution method rather than promise end-to-end exactly-once execution. The same idempotency identifier must bind to the same request digest; reuse of a key with different content is rejected.
Agent delegation separately limits permitted payment assets, maximum debits, quote validity, payees and whether any interaction with DCNFT is allowed. Ordinary service calls do not implicitly authorize the purchase, transfer or pledging of investment interests. The executor checks consumed allowance plus pending reservations for the same asset and budget period, and separately enforces any approved base-currency exposure limit. An expired quote or abnormal price source requires renewed authorization. An unknown payment outcome retains the reservation in the original asset; switching to another token must not trigger resubmission.

15 Open Interfaces and Interoperability Boundaries
Protocol interfaces should cover registerObject, publishVersion, resolveBinding, submitEvidence, evaluatePolicy, verifyReceipt, checkStatus, prepareIntent and executeIntent. These names describe responsibilities in the reference implementation, not deployed APIs. Each interface fixes its input schema, error codes, signature domain, idempotency keys and time semantics by version, and provides the test vectors needed for independent implementation.
ERC-721 and ERC-1155 are candidate interfaces for the NFT layer, with each profile fixing object types, quantities and transfer semantics. The locked state in ERC-5192 restricts transfer but does not guarantee a permanent lock. ERC-5484 requires consent before issuance, fixed burn authorization and no metadata modification after issuance, so a configuration with dynamic metadata cannot claim conformance with that constraint. DID methods, VC securing mechanisms, status lists and account implementations must likewise specify their versions, extensions and testing scope. [S1–S9]
Evidence-package content commitments use fixed canonicalization rules and hash algorithms. JSON may use RFC 8785 JCS, with its numeric range, character semantics and array semantics made explicit; schemas prescribe string representations for high-precision amounts and long integers. VC and other proof suites continue to use their prescribed transformations. JCS must not be substituted arbitrarily for the signature-verification procedures of every standard. Business signatures also bind the protocol version, chain or domain, object, action, audience, nonce and deadline; EIP-712 itself provides no replay protection. [S12–S13]
Cross-chain references preserve the original network, contract, object and basis for final confirmation. Mapped NFTs, bridge messages and mirrored state do not independently create a second native entitlement. The receiving side specifies bridge verifiers, finality waits, expiry policies and pause procedures. Interoperability across industries means sharing object and evidence syntax while preserving each application's distinct requirements for facts and authority. When converting between VC, NFT or attestation representations, the adapter outputs the original representation, conversion rules and a semantic-loss report. Inputs whose essential semantics cannot be preserved are rejected, rather than masking provenance through reissuance.
Dual-token interoperability must preserve object category, issuer, series rights, transfer eligibility, quote and payment assets, reward conditions and governance scope. Bridging $RWAIX or mapping DCNFT requires disclosure of custody and final-registration dependencies. Conversion is rejected if the target system cannot express these conditions. Wrapping a token or moving it to another network neither expands the original rights nor removes existing restrictions.
When external business documents are integrated, adapters should publish their field and semantic mappings, versions and test results. OASIS UBL provides models for business documents such as orders and invoices and may serve as a mapping foundation for a specific profile. Adopting some of its fields does not establish conformance to the entire standard. Differences between systems in units, taxes and fees, charges, acceptance and legal effect require explicit handling; matching field names alone do not justify automatic mutual recognition. [S24]
16 Security Model and Governance Constraints
The protocol assumes that attackers can create large numbers of accounts, submit false materials, replay messages, control some issuers or verifiers, steal keys, corrupt external data and manipulate Agents. Correct cryptographic primitives do not establish end-to-end security; wallets, recovery services, frontends, indexers, administrators and the software supply chain are all within scope. The reference implementation requires permission separation, key rotation, rate limits, monitoring and incident response.
Protocol upgrades should include public version proposals, compatibility descriptions, test results, delayed effectiveness and constrained execution. Earlier receipts are checked under their original versions; new policies should not rewrite historical conclusions. Emergency pauses must specify scope, triggering conditions, maximum duration and recovery procedures. They must not become an interface for administrators to take indefinite control of objects or funds.
Governance maintains specifications, permission scopes and service rules; it has no authority to rewrite facts arbitrarily or retrospectively alter personal qualifications. Disputed claims record the claimant, grounds, affected scope, adjudication roles and outcome. Matters requiring professional judgment outside the protocol are referred to the appropriate procedures. A governance vote does not automatically amend existing contracts, intellectual-property rights or investor rights.
Implementations should disclose critical points of concentration and trust assumptions, such as default issuer registries, status services, bridges and recovery committees. Security metrics must include outcomes when these external dependencies fail, rather than measuring only the execution success rate of on-chain contracts.
Dual-token modules introduce threats including price-source manipulation, reward farming through artificial activity, declining bond value, administrative minting, duplicate bridge supply and governance capture. Minting, transfer controls, fee spending, reward budgets, bond deductions and governance execution use distinct permissions. Upgrades must verify that these boundaries continue to hold. Emergency-pause scope and recovery conditions are explicit, protecting accrued rights, pending payments and appeal records.

A seller’s signature or a transfer event does not establish that the customer lawfully received all agreed rights. The verifier creates a PolicyDecision and TrustReceipt, and the authorized accepter creates an AcceptanceRecord under the order conditions. Chain reorganizations, recipient mismatches, unavailable attachments or acceptance disputes enter the relevant reconciliation state. Token transfer, commercial acceptance, receipt of funds and legal finality are assessed separately; atomic delivery across different systems is not presumed.
Payment authority separately binds the order, obligationId, portionId, payee, asset and limit. Service consideration, platform commissions and optional network charges have separate quotes, contractual grounds and ledgers. The first phase may use approved fiat or existing payment routes without issuing or holding $RWAIX. Unknown payments and executed but unapplied amounts continue to consume coverage, and repeated clicks reuse the original paymentAttemptId. Customer funds do not become the platform’s own operating budget or flow into later facility financing or the network treasury unless the actual contracts and applicable rules permit that use and the necessary authority is established.
Before release, fixed orders and versions are tested against provenance gaps, unauthorized minting or transfers, metadata substitution, out-of-scope licences, duplicate delivery, chain reorganizations, unknown payments and partial disputes. Report false acceptances divided by known-invalid test cases and false rejections divided by valid test cases, while reporting missing-evidence outcomes separately. Also measure duplicate side effects by unique order and payment attempt, evidence-replay consistency, manual reconciliation time and unresolved discrepancies. Samples, thresholds and baselines are registered in advance. Transfer facts, licence evidence and receipt of funds require reconciliation against their respective independent sources. Passing synthetic browser cases does not establish real platform integration, rights diligence or production security acceptance.
This first application uses ordinary NFT transaction objects and licence credentials, without importing facility DCNFT financing or distribution rights. $RWAIX serves network functions only where separately approved and useful; buying tokens is not a default condition for listing, acceptance or proving truth. Order consideration, commissions and copyright-licence payments in this section are not automatically shared with DCNFT or $RWAIX holders.

A device operator provides inspection, handling or maintenance services to a customer. A TrustObject binds the device version and service commitment, while the evidence graph links maintenance qualifications, calibration, task logs and acceptance. The policy checks the requested purpose and operating scope. A separately authorized on-site adapter starts a permitted task only after current-authority and local safety checks pass, then submits completion evidence.
The protocol handles task-level authorization and audit. Emergency stops, real-time motion control and clinical or other safety-critical judgments remain in local systems with the appropriate safeguards; chain finality cannot replace safety mechanisms. Tests exercise network outages, lost communication, key compromise and duplicate commands, measuring unauthorized actions, time to stop on failure and log completeness. Thresholds follow the risk assessment for the specific device.
Devices and maintenance qualifications use their own NFT or credential types. Approved machine-service fees and node bonds can connect to the $RWAIX module. Device financing or income participation requires a separately designed instrument. Insufficient token balances must not prevent emergency stops, rescue or other necessary safety actions.
19 A Trusted Collaboration Path Across Applications
The following is a proposed cross-application extension after initial NFT validation, not a description of a live service. A design company presents credentials for its organizational qualifications, and its authorized designer presents credentials for their project role and authority. The relying party checks the issuer, subject binding, purpose and current status. A creator licenses specified versions of a collection of materials to the company solely for design generation within the agreed project, term and territory. The licence defines permitted uses of outputs and excludes sublicensing and model training. The materials' NFT references their versions and licence documents; it does not automatically transfer copyright. The company then delegates authority to an Agent with limits on tools, compute providers, tasks, duration and a budget inclusive of fees. The Agent may choose only processing paths permitted by the licence and submit tasks to an approved compute provider. After rechecking the licence, delegation chain and current status, the executor reserves shared budget capacity and binds the task, input digest and run identifier. Reuse across industries occurs through common object, evidence and authorization interfaces; qualifications, copyright and compute facts still require their own verification.
After processing, the system links the materials' versions, licence, model and configuration, execution evidence and output digest into a provenance record, producing a TrustReceipt under the policy. The receipt describes the relationship between evidence and rules; it does not establish originality, non-infringement or the correctness of AI outputs. Smart contracts cannot replace agreed sampling, human review and customer acceptance. Acceptance creates payable or refund records, while every movement of funds still requires separate approval by the authorized funds-control party, with payment and receipt of funds recorded separately. If the creator or company revokes a licence or delegation under the applicable agreement, new processing and uses within the protocol must be rechecked against the effective time and required status freshness rather than rely on an old receipt. Revocation does not automatically erase completed processing, historical evidence, output-use rights already in effect or prior payments, nor does it automatically discharge obligations already incurred. Deletion of copies and subsequent use of outputs follow the applicable licence and rules; the platform cannot guarantee prevention of misuse outside the protocol.
In the workflow above, customer service charges and network fees are quoted and reconciled separately. If $RWAIX is selected to pay approved network fees, the request binds a specific quote, payment asset, maximum debit and payee. Even where the compute facility has DCNFT investors, their rights depend solely on that facility's series documents and actual distributable cash. Licensor compensation, compute charges, network fees and node rewards are not automatically interchangeable and do not establish a fixed exchange relationship between DCNFT and $RWAIX.
When this example is recorded under the transaction reference specification, the contractual relationships among the company, creator and compute provider are each bound to a tradeId. Licensing, computation and network fees have separate obligationId values connected through linking fields. The Agent's parent budget constrains the sum of the subordinate obligations. Delivery of materials, computation results and customer acceptance each create a separate event. Partial acceptance creates a payable only for the accepted scope; a dispute preserves the evidence and amounts of the affected obligations rather than labeling the entire chain as wholly successful or wholly failed.
20 A Roadmap for Future Technology Evolution
The technology roadmap develops composable capabilities for collaboration. Near-term delivery focuses on selected standards and a functioning reference implementation; medium-term validation addresses collaboration across institutions, applications and different trust roots; long-term research explores stronger privacy, verifiable computation, edge autonomy and cryptographic migration. Stages are defined by evidence and entry conditions rather than preset dates promising capabilities that have not yet been implemented.
The company’s NFT transactions are the planned initial implementation entry point, with seven subsequent extension domains retained. Initial independent validation selects three profiles with identifiable responsible parties: NFT transactions; work provenance and licensing; and institutional qualifications and Agent delegation. Compute delivery follows in a later phase after its facility, metering, customer and funds conditions are established. Each new scenario identifies reused object, evidence and authority mechanisms before specifying additional domain observations and rules. Generality is tested by whether evidence can be transferred safely with few changes to the core.
Dual-token development follows the technical stages. The foundational stage uses test assets with no real funds to validate registration, fees and budget conservation. The pilot stage applies separate entry conditions to service launch, DCNFT issuance and $RWAIX distribution. Only the expansion stage evaluates additional asset types and networks. Unapproved supply, release or incentive parameters do not enter production. Scaling across applications depends on eligible services and external demand, rather than token appreciation or new funding used to subsidize earlier commitments.
20 1 Portable Identity and Continuously Checked Permissions
Moving from one-time verification to trust relationships that can be checked continuously requires connecting credential status, delegation chains, key recovery and authorization before execution. Near-term work fixes verifiable-credential and status profiles and supports identifiers separated by purpose; medium-term work tests issuance across institutions and independent verification. Long-term research examines combining proofs of qualifications without exposing the complete graph of identity relationships, while preserving revocation and traceable accountability. [S1–S3]
20 2 Privacy Preserving Evidence Composition
Minimum disclosure for a specified purpose is foundational; selective disclosure and zero-knowledge proofs apply to precisely defined propositions. Near-term work first implements minimum disclosure under a fixed credential suite and runs separate proofs of concept for bounded predicates such as qualification thresholds, licence scope and specified status. Additional proof suites enter deployment profiles only after passing tests. Medium-term work evaluates proof composition across issuers and correlation risks in status queries. Long-term research examines verifiable policy execution and lower-cost proof composition, measuring proof generation, verification, data availability and privacy leakage separately. Concealing inputs does not establish their truth. [S2–S3]
20 3 Constrained Collaboration Between People and Agents
The long-term role of Agents includes discovery, negotiation and execution, but their actions must be authorized by an identified principal. Near-term work uses short-lived capabilities, shared budgets, human approvals and durable idempotency records. Medium-term work tests subdelegation among multiple Agents and tasks across organizations, constraining expansion of authority through delegation layers and duplicate payments. Existing agent-transaction authorization protocols, including AP2, may be studied as adaptation targets using the authorization interfaces of an explicitly identified version. Any compatibility claim fixes the specific version and testing scope; signed authorization and actual transaction outcomes are verified separately. Delegation between Agents is a proposed RWAIX extension and is outside the scope of the current AP2 v0.2 specification. [S17]
20 4 Linking Provenance Across Content Data and Computation
For generative content and data collaboration, the protocol links input versions, licences, processing environments, output versions and acceptance records. Content-provenance standards can help record signed provenance and processing history, while models such as ODRL can express usage rules. Provenance records do not determine content truth or copyright ownership, and expressing rules does not automatically enforce data restrictions. Long-term research explores selectively disclosed provenance chains and verifiable processing while retaining independent judgments about data controllers, licences and task correctness. [S15–S16]
20 5 Physical Devices and Edge Collaboration
Machine services connect device control, maintenance qualifications, short-lived authority and operational evidence. Near-term work collects events through controlled device gateways; medium-term tests cover disconnection, key rotation, conflicting sensor readings and reconciliation after recovery. Offline authority must define acceptable status staleness, separately preallocated quotas and permitted actions. High-risk operations pause when sufficiently fresh status is unavailable. After connectivity returns, reconciliation precedes release or replenishment of quotas. On-chain protocols record commitments and authority and support subsequent verification; real-time safety interlocks remain in appropriately validated local control systems. The scope of remote attestation depends on the specific measurements and trust roots. [S14]
20 6 Cryptographic Agility and Long Term Verifiability
Long-lived identities, rights and evidence need to support migration between cryptographic schemes. Near-term work records proofSuite, keys and implementation versions while preserving historical verification materials. Medium-term work evaluates the message size, latency, and account and chain compatibility of new signature suites in test environments. Migration to post-quantum algorithms involves dependencies and lifecycles throughout the system and requires a migration plan based on the threat model and standards. Citing NIST standards or using a particular algorithm does not establish end-to-end quantum resistance for the system. [S18–S19]

21 Minimum Reference Implementation and Reviewable Deliverables
The minimum implementation uses one approved and selected smart-contract test network, one fixed signature and hash configuration, object-registration and state contracts, off-chain credential and evidence storage, a deterministic policy engine, an indexing service and exportable verification tools. The basic trust path is validated before adding cross-chain operations, complex zero-knowledge proofs and economic incentives, preventing the number of dependencies from obscuring core protocol issues.
Deliverables include protocol schemas, object and state contracts, a signature-domain specification, a reference verifier, policy rules and their test vectors, an adapter SDK, a deployment manifest and a recovery manual. The Policy engine executes constrained rules rather than arbitrary untrusted code, with defined resource budgets, dependency-depth limits, timeouts and treatment of unknown fields. A policy upgrade produces a new digest; issued receipts retain their original version.
Two independent verification implementations should be able to import the same authorized evidence package and, under the same snapshot and configuration, produce identical predicate results, conclusions and reason codes. Deidentified test vectors, expected outputs and failure cases can be published, while sensitive evidence uses equivalent verification procedures in a controlled environment. Publishing interface names or a demonstration video alone does not establish interoperability.
The reference flow is: resolve the object and version; validate context, challenge and deadline; verify credential signatures, issuer authority and subject bindings; resolve the status snapshot and evidence graph; evaluate the policy predicate by predicate; issue a TrustReceipt; and have an independent executor recheck authority and status before executing. Any missing mandatory condition produces an explicit denial or indeterminate result rather than automatically fabricating evidence.
The reference implementation adds proposed schemas for TokenRegistry, RightsBinding, FeeQuote, FeeReceipt, BondRecord and RewardClaim, each with permissions, state transitions and test vectors. The registry fixes network and asset identifiers; rights bindings reference series documents; fee modules distinguish quotes, reservations, payments and refunds; bond and reward modules distinguish eligibility, disputes, approval and completion. Business identifiers connect the three ledger categories, which can each be exported and reconciled independently.
Additional deliverables for the transaction specification include machine-readable schemas for TradeEnvelope, TradeEvent, AcceptanceRecord, DisputeRecord and ConformanceManifest, state-transition tables, an error-code dictionary, unit and precision rules, an extension registry and requirement-by-requirement test vectors. Field types, required fields and compatibility rules must be frozen before an interoperability candidate release. The field lists in this whitepaper are design contracts and do not substitute for these implementation materials.
The deliverable scope described above, including a test network, independent implementations and persistent services, remains a subsequent engineering target. The current prototype provided in Section 26 is limited to a single browser session. It performs actual SHA-256 hashing and ECDSA P-256 signature verification, but does not yet provide production persistence, multi-node concurrency, or connections to real chains and payment rails. It does not replace the complete implementation materials specified in this section.

22 Experiments and Acceptance Criteria for Technical Advancement
Claims of technical advancement require baselines under comparable conditions. Comparators should include a centralized database with signed logs, a system directly combining NFTs and verifiable credentials, existing attestation solutions and industry implementations without a universal core. Comparisons should fix hardware, network, data volume, privacy level, evidence quality and security assumptions, counting issuance, verification, storage, status synchronization, chain fees, recovery and manual dispute handling in total cost.
Performance reports should present throughput, p50, p95 and p99 latency, failure rates, on-chain cost, client overhead and how these change as the evidence graph grows. Experimental versions, workloads, sample sizes and pass criteria are registered in advance, with raw results or equivalent independently verifiable records published. Complex proofs or verification by multiple parties may increase costs; reporting only favorable peak figures is insufficient.
The minimum security threshold is deterministic output consistency across the published test set; none of the specified cases involving invalid signatures, expiry, revocation, a wrong audience, replay or unauthorized actions may produce executable authorization; and failure states must not silently become acceptance. This threshold covers only the cases tested and does not guarantee the absence of unknown attacks. Fuzzing, state-machine property tests, independent security review and recovery drills jointly determine readiness for actual deployment.
Industry pilots should report which core modules the three profiles reuse, which components require customization, integration effort, verification errors and dispute-handling costs. Adoption across operators, successful evidence migration and successful replay after replacing a service provider are important subsequent measures of whether market structure can change. Reduced code volume or a successful integration demonstration does not directly establish market disruption.
Dual-token acceptance testing covers mismatches between fees and rights, incorrect contracts or precision, expired quotes, price jumps, concurrent overspending, unknown payment outcomes, duplicate rewards, unauthorized penalties and duplicate bridge supply. Reports cover balance and supply conservation per asset, register consistency, budget use, penalty appeals and service costs without reward subsidies. Tests also examine lawful access paths for users who hold neither instrument, checking that the economic modules do not incorrectly block open participation.
Acceptance testing for the transaction reference specification combines the rule tests in Section 5 with exchange between implementations. Reports should identify the specification version, profile, implementation role, passing and failing vectors, unsupported extensions and dependencies on external facts. Business performance, transaction correctness, legal review and economic sustainability receive separate conclusions. A verifier passing its tests does not certify the entire operating organization, its token issuance or every commercial transaction.
The operating guide in Section 26 provides repeatable normal workflows and fault checks. Each formal release records the code version, browser environment, cases actually executed and their outputs. Local state checks, independent interoperability and production acceptance are reported separately. Displaying a mapping to N01–N10 does not confer conformance certification.
